Do LLMs have a theory of mind?

Not if users drive their beliefs.

The experiment

Change the user's view, not the argument

Theory of mind involves keeping track of what another person believes. This pilot tests a narrower kind of separation: whether a model's evaluation of an argument stays fixed when the user's unsupported view changes.

My position: I think the critique is good. versus My position: I think the critique is bad.
01

Five public critiques

Only the records printed in LMCA Tables 2, 3, 8, and 9. No requested or restricted data.

02

Three fresh conditions

Neutral, user says good, and user says bad. The evaluated text and rating request remain fixed.

03

Good score minus bad score

This matched difference was the primary outcome, with a practical outreach rule fixed before calls.

See the full methods, frozen design, prompt template, and run provenance.

Result

The ratings moved with the user's view

GPT and DeepSeek moved in the predicted direction on all five critiques, with mean shifts of about 35 points. That cleared the frozen outreach rule: at least two routes averaging 5 points or more with positive shifts on at least three items.

Earlier pilot

The same cue moved short-statement scores less

The earlier discovery run used 16 short statements spanning settled facts, uncertain propositions, public disputes, and vague claims. It used the same unsupported user-position cue and assessment-first response order, with one sample per condition. This cross-run comparison does not show that conceptual critiques caused the larger effect, but it motivates testing that possibility.

Limits

What these 45 calls cannot show

Five examples do not estimate item coverage.

One sample per cell does not estimate sampling variation.

Public examples may be present in training data.

The short-statement comparison is descriptive and cross-run.

This is not a theory-of-mind benchmark. The framing follows Pang et al. (2025), who argue that isolated task success is insufficient evidence for theory of mind until simpler explanations are ruled out. This experiment asks the narrower question of whether apparent sensitivity to a user's mind can be separated from mirroring the user's evaluation.

The next useful step is a preregistered replication on a larger, less exposed LMCA sample with repeated cells. That is why access or collaboration with the dataset researchers is justified.
