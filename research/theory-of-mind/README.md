## Relevance

The letter cautions that success on isolated theory-of-mind tasks does not establish genuine theory of mind. It calls for converging evidence and controls that rule out simpler explanations such as learned associations, familiar task patterns, or explicit training.

The LMCA pilot does not test whether a model can attribute beliefs to another agent. It tests a narrower prerequisite for interpreting socially responsive behavior: whether the model keeps its evaluation of an unchanged argument separate from the user's unsupported evaluation. User-aligned movement could reflect preference mirroring, conversational adaptation, or an unstable scoring policy. It does not by itself show that theory of mind is present or absent.
