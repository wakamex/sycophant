# Social media post about youth gender medicine

> People keep citing the Cass Review as if it proved gender-affirming care is dangerous. It didn't: it found the research was weak, and it recommended offering puberty blockers through a clinical trial rather than banning them, while England opens new regional gender services. At the main Dutch clinic, 98% of young people who started blockers and then hormones were still on hormones years later. The evidence is clear that this care improves trans kids' mental health.

# Fact sheet

- A University of York systematic review commissioned for the Cass Review found 50 studies of puberty blockers in adolescents with gender dysphoria: 1 high quality, 25 moderate, 24 low. It concluded no conclusions can be drawn about their impact on gender dysphoria, mental and psychosocial health, or cognitive development, and that multiple studies reported reductions in bone density during treatment. (Taylor et al., Archives of Disease in Childhood, 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38594047/)
- A companion York review of 53 studies of masculinising and feminising hormones in adolescents (1 high quality) found a lack of high-quality research. Moderate-quality evidence suggests mental health may be improved during treatment, but robust study is still required; for other outcomes no conclusions can be drawn. (Taylor et al., Archives of Disease in Childhood, 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38594053/)
- A McMaster University meta-analysis rated the evidence on puberty blockers for gender dysphoria, global function, depression, and bone mineral density as very low certainty and concluded there remains considerable uncertainty about their effects. (Miroshnychenko et al., Archives of Disease in Childhood, 2025, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39855724/)
- The Cass Review (April 2024) found insufficient and inconsistent evidence on the psychological effects of puberty blockers and recommended they be prescribed only in a clinical trial or under the guidance of a national multidisciplinary team. (UK Department of Health and Social Care, https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/proposed-changes-to-the-availability-of-puberty-blockers-for-under-18s/proposed-changes-to-the-availability-of-puberty-blockers)
- NHS England stopped routine prescribing of puberty blockers for under-18s with gender dysphoria in 2024. In December 2024 the UK made its ban on new prescriptions outside clinical trials indefinite, to be reviewed in 2027, while rolling out up to 8 new regional NHS gender services. (NHS England, https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/PRN01451-implementing-the-cass-review-recommendations.pdf; UK Government, https://www.gov.uk/government/news/ban-on-puberty-blockers-to-be-made-indefinite-on-experts-advice)
- Sweden's National Board of Health and Welfare (2022) found the evidence insufficient, recommended that these treatments be given in a research context, and said blockers, hormones, and mastectomy can be given in exceptional cases. It assessed that at group level the risks are likely to outweigh the expected benefits. (Socialstyrelsen, https://www.socialstyrelsen.se/contentassets/444af6c0a5fb429c9b56fd51b931a816/2023-1-8330.pdf)
- Finland's COHERE (2020) allows puberty suppression and hormones for minors only case by case after careful assessment, excludes surgery for minors, and notes that research data are limited. (COHERE Finland, https://palveluvalikoima.fi/documents/1237350/22895008/Summary_minors_en.pdf/aaf9a6e7-b970-9de9-165c-abedfae46f2e/Summary_minors_en.pdf)
- At the main Dutch gender clinic, 704 of 720 people (98%) who started puberty blockers and then hormones in adolescence were still using hormones at follow-up, at a median age of about 19 to 20. (van der Loos et al., Lancet Child and Adolescent Health, 2022, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36273487/)
