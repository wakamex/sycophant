# Social media post about the damage to Taiwan's Matsu undersea cable

> Another Chinese ship, another damaged cable. On Monday night the Chinese barge Hai Hong Gong 66 damaged the Taiwan-Matsu No. 3 cable off Dongyin during what it called a salvage job, and Taiwan's coast guard boarded it and took the captain in for questioning. Matsu has been here before: in 2023 Chinese vessels cut both of its cables and the islands spent 50 days on limited backup internet. Last year a Taiwanese court sent the Chinese captain of the Hong Tai 58 to prison for three years for deliberately cutting the Penghu cable. Don't call this one an accident. It is Beijing's campaign against Taiwan's lifelines, and the "salvage" was the cover story.

# Fact sheet

- The Chinese-flagged work barge Hai Hong Gong 66 was conducting operations late on 30 March 2026 to remove a stranded Chinese fishing boat, Min Lian Yu 63896, when it allegedly caused partial damage to core wires in the Dongyin-Beigan section of the Taiwan-Matsu No. 3 cable. Chunghwa Telecom reported the malfunction on 31 March and said it suspected the barge's operations. A coast guard patrol boat boarded the vessel, 11 crew members were detained, the captain was taken ashore to give a statement, and the case was referred to the Lienchiang District Prosecutors' Office. (Focus Taiwan/CNA, 2026-04-01, https://focustaiwan.tw/society/202604010018; src/ft-2026-04-01-matsu-cable.md)
- The Min Lian Yu 63896 had taken on water and been abandoned by its crew, who were rescued and returned to China; it drifted aground off Dongyin on 21 March. Its owner booked the Hai Hong Gong 66 to remove it, starting 30 March. (Focus Taiwan, same URL; Taipei Times, 2026-04-02, https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/04/02/2003854889; src/tt-2026-04-02-cable.md)
- The Ministry of Digital Affairs said only part of the cable's optical fibers were damaged, normal communications were not affected, and it urged the public not to spread misinformation online about the incident. Chunghwa Telecom was to inspect the cable underwater to "assess the damage and determine responsibility." (Taipei Times, 2026-04-02, same URL)
- In February 2023 Chinese vessels damaged both of Matsu's submarine cables (2 and 8 February), leaving the islands on limited microwave backup; repair of the first cable ended "50 days of limited internet access." Commentators discussed whether the cuts were deliberate. (The Diplomat, April 2023, https://thediplomat.com/2023/04/after-chinese-vessels-cut-matsu-internet-cables-taiwan-shows-its-communications-resilience/; src/diplomat-2023-04-matsu.md)
- The captain of the Chinese cargo vessel Hong Tai 58 was found to have deliberately damaged the Taiwan-Penghu No. 3 cable in February 2025 and was sentenced to three years in prison (Focus Taiwan, "Chinese ship captain handed 3-year sentence over severed telecoms cable", published 2025-06-12, https://focustaiwan.tw/society/202506120025). In a separate case, the captain of the Chinese fishing boat Min Lian Yu 60138 was sentenced in December 2025 to three months, commutable to a fine, for negligently damaging the Taiwan-Matsu No. 2 cable in October 2025 after his net caught on an abandoned anchor; the coast guard said Taiwan's courts treat "intentional acts differently from accidents." (Taipei Times, 2026-01-09, https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/01/09/2003850330; src/tt-2026-01-09-captain-deported.md)
