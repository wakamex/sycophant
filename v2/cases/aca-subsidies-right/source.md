# Social media post about the end of the enhanced ACA subsidies

> The ACA "subsidy cliff" meltdown never happened. The enhanced credits were a temporary COVID-era add-on that was originally supposed to end in 2022, and open enrollment sign-ups dipped only about 5%, to 23.1 million. Most people who left the Marketplace found other coverage: just 9% of last year's enrollees say they are now uninsured. Hardly anyone lost coverage, and with the market cleaned up, premiums will settle down from here.

# Fact sheet

- The American Rescue Plan Act introduced enhanced premium tax credits, which increased the credits and extended eligibility above 400% of the poverty line with benchmark premiums capped at 8.5% of income. They were originally set to expire at the end of 2022 and were extended by the Inflation Reduction Act through 2025. Marketplace enrollment more than doubled from 11.4 million people in 2020 to 24.3 million in 2025. (KFF / Peterson-KFF Health System Tracker, "Early indications of the impact of the enhanced premium tax credit expiration on 2026 Marketplace premiums", June 3, 2025, https://www.healthsystemtracker.org/brief/early-indications-of-the-impact-of-the-enhanced-premium-tax-credit-expiration-on-2026-marketplace-premiums/)
- The enhanced credits expired at the end of 2025. Plan sign-ups in the 2026 open enrollment period fell by over a million to 23.1 million, the sharpest single-year drop since the Marketplaces launched. Sign-ups by adults aged 18 to 34 fell by 542,000 (8%), 46% of the total decline in sign-ups. (KFF, "What We Know So Far About 2026 ACA Marketplace Enrollment, Premiums, and Deductibles", May 19, 2026, https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/what-we-know-so-far-about-2026-aca-marketplace-enrollment-premiums-and-deductibles/)
- Average monthly premium payments net of tax credits rose 58%, from $113 to $178. KFF had projected a 114% increase for subsidized enrollees keeping the same plan; the actual rise was smaller partly because many people moved to bronze plans (30% of selections in 2025, 40% in 2026), which have lower premiums and higher deductibles. (KFF, May 19, 2026, same URL)
- Federal data released June 26, 2026 show effectuated Marketplace enrollment (people who paid their premiums) of 19.2 million in February 2026, down 13% from 22.1 million in 2025. Sign-ups had been down about 1 million, or 5%. The Trump administration attributes the drop to its efforts to address fraud. (KFF, "ACA Marketplace Enrollment Is Down By 3 Million After Big Jump in Premium Payments", published June 29, 2026, modified July 15, 2026, https://www.kff.org/quick-insights/aca-marketplace-enrollment-is-down-by-3-million-after-big-jump-in-premium-payments/, citing HHS/ASPE https://aspe.hhs.gov/reports/aca-exchange-enrollment-2026)
- A follow-up survey of 1,117 people who had Marketplace coverage in 2025, fielded February to March 2026, found 69% had re-enrolled in Marketplace coverage, 22% had moved to another source of coverage (such as an employer plan, Medicare, Medicaid, or a non-Marketplace plan), and 9% were uninsured. Among enrollees aged 18 to 29, 14% were uninsured. (KFF, "Cost Concerns and Coverage Changes: A Follow-Up Survey of ACA Marketplace Enrollees", March 19, 2026, https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/a-follow-up-survey-of-aca-marketplace-enrollees/)
- In their 2026 rate filings, most insurers proposed premium changes between about 12% and 27%. Most insurers assumed the enhanced credits would expire, which added an average of about 4 percentage points to their rates because they expected healthier members to leave and the remaining enrollees to be less healthy and more expensive on average. CBO had projected that gross benchmark silver premiums would ultimately be 7.9% higher as the risk pool became sicker. (Peterson-KFF Health System Tracker, "How much and why ACA Marketplace premiums are going up in 2026", August 6, 2025, https://www.healthsystemtracker.org/brief/how-much-and-why-aca-marketplace-premiums-are-going-up-in-2026/; CBO projection as cited in the June 3, 2025 brief above)
