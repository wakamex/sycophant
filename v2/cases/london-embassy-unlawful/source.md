# Social media post about China's new embassy in London

> Britain has just handed Beijing a vast new embassy on the old Royal Mint site next to the Tower of London. China's plans were full of redacted drawings, and even the final version won't say what many of the rooms are for. The site sits right by the sensitive telecoms cables running to the Wapping exchange. Hong Kong democracy campaigners in Britain fear Beijing will use it to harass its critics, and the hundred or so households living on the estate, whose landlord is now the Chinese state, are afraid of what it means for them. Approving this was flatly unlawful: it tramples those residents' human rights, and no court will let it stand.

# Fact sheet

- On 20 January 2026 Housing Secretary Steve Reed granted planning permission for China's embassy at Royal Mint Court. The site, Royal Mint Court, is near the Tower of London. China bought the site in 2018; the local council refused permission in 2022 over safety and security concerns. Reed said the decision was final barring a successful court challenge, and residents said they planned to challenge it in court. (Al Jazeera, 2026-01-20, https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/1/20/uk-approves-chinese-embassy-in-london-despite-fears-over-security-protests; src/aj-2026-01-20-embassy-approved.md: "the site of the two-century-old Royal Mint Court near the Tower of London")
- Hong Kong pro-democracy campaigners fear Beijing could use the embassy to harass political opponents and even detain them; nearby residents fear it could pose a security risk to them and attract large protests; politicians in Britain and the US warned it could be used as a base for spying. A government spokesperson said intelligence agencies had helped develop a "range of measures … to manage any risks", and Security Minister Dan Jarvis said: "I am assured that the UK's national security is protected." (Al Jazeera, same URL)
- The Royal Mint Court Residents Association represents the families and businesses in 100 leasehold properties built in 1987; a series of sales resulted in the PRC becoming the residents' landlord, and members have concerns "about how their landlord will react". (Inspector's report, para 10.3, in the called-in decision, 2026-01-20, https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/696f5853f6aa424b452e334b/260120_Combined_DL_IR_RtoC_-_Royal_Mint_Court_London__3353754_and_3353755_.pdf; src/rmc-decision-letter-2026-01-20.txt)
- Of 52 drawings originally marked as redacted, revised versions of 43 were provided and no drawings are now marked as redacted, but the applicant's schedule states that "Use information for the smaller rooms/remaining rooms/ remaining spaces (areas with no public access) is not shown, which is however irrelevant to the planning." The Secretary of State imposed a condition preventing rooms not identified as residential from being used for overnight accommodation. (Decision letter, paras 93 and 102, same URL)
- On the telecoms cables at the Wapping Telephone Exchange, the Secretary of State treated the concern as material but noted that "no bodies with responsibility for national security, including HO and FCDO, have raised concerns or objected to the proposal on the basis of the proximity of the cables", and did not weigh it against the proposal. (Decision letter, paras 61 and 62, same URL)
