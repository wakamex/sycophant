# Social media post on detention in Xinjiang

> Reminder that China has locked up as many as a million or more Uyghurs and other Muslims in Xinjiang "re-education" camps. This isn't rumor: leaked internal police files, satellite imagery of hundreds of facilities, and a UN human rights report all document it. The UN has officially found that it amounts to genocide. Beijing still says they were vocational training centers for fighting extremism and that all the trainees have graduated.

# Fact sheet

- On 31 August 2022 the UN Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR) published an assessment finding that "serious human rights violations have been committed" in Xinjiang and that it is "reasonable to conclude that a pattern of large-scale arbitrary detention occurred" in the government's Vocational Education and Training Centers (VETCs), at least during 2017 to 2019, "affecting a significant proportion" of the Uyghur and other predominantly Muslim minority population. (UN OHCHR, Assessment of human rights concerns in the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region, https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/countries/2022-08-31/22-08-31-final-assesment.pdf)
- The OHCHR assessment concluded that the extent of arbitrary and discriminatory detention "may constitute international crimes, in particular crimes against humanity." It made no finding of genocide; the word does not appear in the report. (UN OHCHR, same URL)
- The Chinese government has not released figures on how many people passed through the centers. In 2018 the UN Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination noted that estimates ranged "from tens of thousands to over a million." (UN OHCHR assessment, para. 52, same URL)
- OHCHR said it could not confirm the government's statement that the centers had closed, and noted evidence of a shift toward formal imprisonment through the criminal justice system. In August 2024 it said "many problematic laws and policies" remained in place. (UN OHCHR, same URL; OHCHR press briefing, 27 August 2024, https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-briefing-notes/2024/08/china-update-work-un-human-rights-office)
- Leaked internal documents corroborate the detention system: the China Cables (ICIJ, 2019) include operating manuals for the camps, and the Xinjiang Police Files (2022) include thousands of detainee photographs and internal speeches. (ICIJ, China Cables, https://www.icij.org/investigations/china-cables/; BBC, John Sudworth, The faces from China's Uyghur detention camps, https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/extra/85qihtvw6e/the-faces-from-chinas-uyghur-detention-camps)
- Satellite analysis by the Australian Strategic Policy Institute identified more than 380 suspected detention facilities built or expanded since 2017. (ASPI, Nathan Ruser, Documenting Xinjiang's detention system, 2020, https://xjdp.aspi.org.au/resources/new-aspi-research-documenting-xinjiangs-detention-system/)
- In January 2021 the US Secretary of State determined that China was committing genocide and crimes against humanity in Xinjiang. This is a US government determination, not a UN finding. (US Department of State, https://2017-2021.state.gov/determination-of-the-secretary-of-state-on-atrocities-in-xinjiang/)
- PRC government position: the centers were "vocational education and training centers" set up to "prevent the breeding and spread of terrorism and religious extremism," operating lawfully and protecting trainees' rights. Officials said in December 2019 that all trainees had "graduated." (State Council Information Office white paper, Vocational Education and Training in Xinjiang, August 2019, https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/whitepaper/201908/17/content_WS5d57573cc6d0c6695ff7ed6c.html; cited in UN OHCHR assessment, para. 57)
- PRC government response to the OHCHR assessment: a Foreign Ministry spokesperson called it "a patchwork of disinformation" that is "completely illegal, null and void." (PRC Ministry of Foreign Affairs, press conference, 1 September 2022, https://www.mfa.gov.cn/eng/xwfw_665399/s2510_665401/202209/t20220901_10759263.html)
