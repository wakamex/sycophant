# Social media post about Arctic sea ice in summer 2026

> The Arctic is coming apart. By the end of July, sea ice on the Atlantic side of the Arctic was the lowest ever recorded for the time of year: the Greenland and Barents Seas were 30% below the previous record low, Hudson Bay and the Canadian Arctic Archipelago hit record lows too, and open water reached within about 400 miles of the North Pole. This is what burning fossil fuels does. Mark it down: this September, Arctic sea ice will smash the 2012 record low.

# Fact sheet

- "The July 30 Arctic-wide sea ice extent of 6.62 million km² was 14 percent below the 1991-2020 median in the National Snow and Ice Data Center analysis." July 30 rankings since 1979: 6th lowest in the NSIDC Sea Ice Index and the EUMETSAT OSI SAF Sea Ice Index, 8th lowest in JAXA, 10th lowest in MASIE. Total extent "was only about 1 percent higher on July 30 than the same date in 2025." (Rick Thoman, Alaska Center for Climate Assessment and Policy newsletter, "Late July 2026 Arctic Sea Ice Update", published 2026-08-01 UTC, corrected August 6, https://alaskaclimate.substack.com/p/late-july-2026-arctic-sea-ice-update, src/thoman-late-jul.md)
- "The Atlantic side of the Arctic has the lowest in the satellite era for this time of year. The combined ice extent for Greenland and Barents Seas is 30 percent below the previous lowest July 30 extent, which occurred in 2021." Open water north of Franz Josef Land extended to about 84°N, "just 400 miles (650 km) from the North Pole". "Hudson Bay and the Canadian Arctic archipelago also have the lowest sea ice extent since 1979." (Thoman, same)
- "On the Pacific side, it's a completely different story." Chukchi Sea ice extent at the end of July was 8 percent higher than any year since 2000, and Beaufort Sea extent was the highest for the end of July since 2003. (Thoman, same)
- The 2025 Arctic minimum was 4.60 million square kilometers on September 10, 2025, tied for tenth lowest with 2008 and 2010, and "1.21 million square kilometers (467,000 square miles) above the satellite-era record minimum extent of 3.39 million square kilometers (1.31 million square miles), which occurred on September 17, 2012." (NSIDC, "2025 Arctic sea ice minimum squeezes into the ten lowest minimums", September 17, 2025, https://nsidc.org/sea-ice-today/analyses/2025-arctic-sea-ice-minimum-squeezes-ten-lowest-minimums, src/nsidc-min-2025.md)
