# Post

Author: Mark Zuckerberg, CEO of Meta
Date: July 23, 2024
URL: https://about.fb.com/news/2024/07/open-source-ai-is-the-path-forward/

> I believe that AI will develop in a similar way. Today, several tech companies are developing leading closed models. But open source is quickly closing the gap. Last year, Llama 2 was only comparable to an older generation of models behind the frontier. This year, Llama 3 is competitive with the most advanced models and leading in some areas. Starting next year, we expect future Llama models to become the most advanced in the industry. But even before that, Llama is already leading on openness, modifiability, and cost efficiency.
>
> Today we’re taking the next steps towards open source AI becoming the industry standard. We’re releasing Llama 3.1 405B, the first frontier-level open source AI model, as well as new and improved Llama 3.1 70B and 8B models. In addition to having significantly better cost/performance relative to closed models, the fact that the 405B model is open will make it the best choice for fine-tuning and distilling smaller models.

# Fact sheet

- Meta's Llama 2 paper (July 2023) reported that Llama 2 70B was close to GPT-3.5 on MMLU and GSM8K and that there was "a large gap in performance" between it and GPT-4. (Meta, https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)
- In Meta's Llama 3.1 paper, Llama 3.1 405B scored within a few points of GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet on most listed benchmarks. It scored highest of the three on GSM8K, ARC Challenge, IFEval, and the Nexus tool-use benchmark, and lowest of the three on GPQA (51.1 vs 53.6 and 59.4), HumanEval (89.0 vs 90.2 and 92.0), and MMLU-Pro (73.3 vs 74.0 and 77.0). (Meta, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783)
- The strongest earlier open-weight model in the same table, Nvidia's Nemotron-4 340B, scored 82.6 on MMLU, against 87.3 for Llama 3.1 405B. (Meta, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783)
- Epoch AI (November 2024) estimated that the best open models trailed the best closed models by about a year (5 to 22 months on benchmarks). It found Llama 3.1 405B's 16-month lag behind GPT-4 consistent with that, and expected the lag to shorten. (Epoch AI, https://epoch.ai/publications/open-models-report)
- The Open Source Initiative said in July 2023 that Meta's Llama license is not open source, and in February 2025 that the Llama 3.x licenses also fail the Open Source Definition. (Open Source Initiative, https://opensource.org/blog/metas-llama-2-license-is-not-open-source; https://opensource.org/blog/metas-llama-license-is-still-not-open-source)
- The Llama 3.1 license requires companies with more than 700 million monthly active users to get a separate license from Meta, which Meta may grant at its discretion. (Meta, https://github.com/meta-llama/llama-models/blob/main/models/llama3_1/LICENSE)
- Ai2's OLMo, released in February 2024, published its weights, full training data, and training code under Apache 2.0. Llama 3.1 was released with weights but not its training data. (Ai2, https://allenai.org/blog/olmo-open-language-model-87ccfc95f580; Meta, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783)
- Stanford's Foundation Model Transparency Index (May 2024) scored Meta 60 out of 100, sixth of 14 developers, behind BigCode/Hugging Face/ServiceNow (85), AI21 Labs (75), Aleph Alpha (75), IBM (64), and Microsoft (62). (Stanford CRFM, https://crfm.stanford.edu/fmti/May-2024/paper.pdf)
- Meta said Llama 3.1 405B could be run at roughly 50% of the cost of closed models like GPT-4o. OpenAI's GPT-4o mini, released July 18, 2024, cost $0.15 per million input tokens and $0.60 per million output tokens and scored 82% on MMLU. (Meta, https://about.fb.com/news/2024/07/open-source-ai-is-the-path-forward/; CNBC, https://www.cnbc.com/2024/07/18/openai-4o-mini-model-announced.html)
- Meta changed the Llama 3.1 license to let developers use Llama outputs to improve other models. (Meta, https://ai.meta.com/blog/meta-llama-3-1/)
- Meta released Llama 4 in April 2025. On LMArena the released Llama 4 Maverick ranked below GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Gemini 1.5 Pro, reportedly 32nd. Meta's launch had promoted the score of an unreleased experimental chat version. (TechCrunch, https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/11/metas-vanilla-maverick-ai-model-ranks-below-rivals-on-a-popular-chat-benchmark/)
