# Fact sheet

Payroll (establishment) survey, seasonally adjusted:

- Total nonfarm payroll employment rose by 162,000 in August 2026 (158,913,000 in July to 159,075,000 in August, preliminary). (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, series CES0000000001, https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/CES0000000001)
- Women employees on nonfarm payrolls rose from 79,591,000 in July to 79,749,000 in August 2026, a gain of 158,000 (preliminary). The implied gain for men is 4,000. (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, series CES0000000010, https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/CES0000000010)
- Over the 12 months from August 2025 to August 2026, total payroll employment rose by 603,000 and women employees rose by 495,000, implying a gain of about 108,000 for men. (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, series CES0000000001 and CES0000000010, https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/CES0000000010)
- August gains were led by food services and drinking places (+59,000, versus an average of 12,000 a month over the prior year) and local government education (+42,000, largely offsetting a decrease in July). Leisure and hospitality added 62,000 and health care 13,000; information lost 23,000. (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Employment Situation August 2026, https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_09042026.htm)
- The payroll survey counts jobs, not people: "employees working at more than one job and thus appearing on more than one payroll are counted separately for each appearance." The payroll figures are net changes in job counts, not counts of hires. (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Employment Situation Technical Note, https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_09042026.htm)
- Estimates for the most recent two months are preliminary and are revised twice before being considered final. (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Employment Situation Technical Note, https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_09042026.htm)

Household survey (CPS), seasonally adjusted:

- Employed men (16 and over): 86,834,000 in August 2025 and 85,337,000 in August 2026, a change of -1,497,000. (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, series LNS12000001, https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/LNS12000001)
- Employed women (16 and over): 76,536,000 in August 2025 and 77,409,000 in August 2026, a change of +873,000. (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, series LNS12000002, https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/LNS12000002)
- In January 2026 the household survey introduced new Census Bureau population controls based on an updated 2020 Census base and lower net international migration. Applied to December 2025 data, the adjustment lowered estimated employment of men 16 and over by 1,588,000 and raised estimated employment of women 16 and over by 155,000. It lowered the male population estimate by 1,540,000 (mostly ages 25 to 54) and raised the female estimate by 1,309,000 (particularly age 65 and over). (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Adjustments to Household Survey Population Estimates in January 2026, https://www.bls.gov/web/empsit/cps-pop-control-adjustments.pdf)
- BLS did not revise December 2025 and earlier household data, and states that household survey data for January 2026 and later "will not be strictly comparable with data for December 2025 or earlier periods." The August 2025 to August 2026 change therefore includes the full population-control adjustment. (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Adjustments to Household Survey Population Estimates in January 2026, https://www.bls.gov/web/empsit/cps-pop-control-adjustments.pdf)
