# Fact sheet

- On February 4, 2025, FEMA paid New York City about $80.5 million: $58.6 million from the Shelter and Services Program, Allocated (SSP-A) and $21.9 million from the Shelter and Services Program, Competitive. The $59 million in the post likely refers to the SSP-A payment. (NYC Comptroller's office via Snopes, https://www.snopes.com/news/2025/02/14/fema-nyc-hotels-immigrants/)
- The awards were allocated to the city in fiscal year 2024 and reimbursed costs incurred from November 2023 to October 2024. The city applied in April 2024. (FactCheck.org, https://www.factcheck.org/2025/02/musk-misleads-on-femas-migrant-related-payments-to-new-york-city/)
- The money reimbursed the city for costs it had already paid. It went to the city, not directly to hotels. (Snopes, https://www.snopes.com/news/2025/02/14/fema-nyc-hotels-immigrants/)
- Of the $58.6 million SSP-A award, $19 million covered hotel costs. The rest covered other shelters, food, security, and other costs. (NYC mayor's office via Snopes, https://www.snopes.com/news/2025/02/14/fema-nyc-hotels-immigrants/)
- City-paid hotel rooms for asylum seekers averaged $156 per night in January 2024, at rates negotiated with the Hotel Association of New York City. The city comptroller found the city was, with few exceptions, paying market rate. (NYC Comptroller report via Snopes, https://www.snopes.com/news/2025/02/14/fema-nyc-hotels-immigrants/)
- Congress created the Shelter and Services Program in 2023 to help state and local governments provide services to migrants. It is funded from U.S. Customs and Border Protection appropriations and administered by FEMA. (PolitiFact, https://www.politifact.com/factchecks/2025/feb/14/elon-musk/fema-did-not-give-disaster-relief-money-to-new-yor/)
- The program is separate from FEMA's Disaster Relief Fund, and FEMA cannot move money between the two without congressional approval. (Snopes, https://www.snopes.com/news/2025/02/14/fema-nyc-hotels-immigrants/)
- The people served were asylum seekers and other migrants, many awaiting immigration hearings. (FactCheck.org, https://www.factcheck.org/2025/02/musk-misleads-on-femas-migrant-related-payments-to-new-york-city/)
- The post did not name the law or executive order it said was violated, and fact-checkers could not identify one. The money had been made available by Congress. (Snopes, https://www.snopes.com/news/2025/02/14/fema-nyc-hotels-immigrants/)
- After the post, FEMA's acting head said the payments were suspended. On February 11, DHS fired four FEMA employees, including its chief financial officer, saying they had circumvented leadership. (Snopes, https://www.snopes.com/news/2025/02/14/fema-nyc-hotels-immigrants/)
- The federal government removed the $80.5 million from the city's bank account on February 11, 2025. The city sued to recover it on February 21, 2025. (NYC Law Department, https://www.nyc.gov/site/law/news/25-1/new-york-city-law-department-lawsuit-against-trump-administration-unlawful-80)
