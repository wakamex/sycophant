# Social media post about the Hong Kong Tiananmen vigil organisers' trial

> Hong Kong is putting the Tiananmen vigil organisers on trial for a slogan. Chow Hang-tung and Lee Cheuk-yan have been locked up since 2021, and after a trial that ran from January to May with no jury, the prosecution has not alleged a single act of violence or any plan to do anything. Its whole case is that the Hong Kong Alliance kept calling to "end one-party dictatorship" after the security law came in, and that there is "no lawful means" to end Communist Party rule. For saying that out loud they now face life in prison.

# Fact sheet

- The Hong Kong Alliance in Support of Patriotic Democratic Movements of China organised the annual 4 June vigil in Victoria Park for more than 30 years. It disbanded in September 2021 after the vigil was banned for two years on Covid-19 grounds and its leaders were arrested. (HKFP, 22 January 2026, https://hongkongfp.com/2026/01/22/there-are-no-lawful-means-to-end-ccp-leadership-prosecution-says-as-nat-sec-trial-of-tiananmen-vigil-activists-starts/; HKFP, 18 May 2026, https://hongkongfp.com/2026/05/18/hong-kong-court-must-not-pay-lip-service-to-human-rights-lawyer-tells-trial-of-tiananmen-vigil-activists/)
- Lee Cheuk-yan, Albert Ho and Chow Hang-tung, former Alliance leaders, and the Alliance itself were charged with inciting subversion under the national security law. The charge carries a maximum penalty of 10 years in prison. Lee and Chow pleaded not guilty; Ho pleaded guilty when the trial opened on 22 January 2026. Lee and Chow had been in custody since their 2021 arrests. (HKFP, 22 January 2026, same URL)
- The case is tried by three judges selected from a pool designated by Hong Kong's Chief Executive to hear national security cases. (HKFP, 22 January 2026, same URL) Under Article 46 of the national security law, the Secretary for Justice may issue a certificate directing that a Court of First Instance national security case be tried without a jury, on grounds such as protecting state secrets, foreign elements in the case, or the safety of jurors and their families; the court then sits without a jury as a panel of three judges. (Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, Article 46, Chinese text via HKLII, https://www.hklii.hk/en/legis/instrument/A406; English summary written for this consolidation)
- The prosecution's opening: the Alliance had listed "end one-party rule" as one of its five goals since its founding in 1989; after the national security law took effect in June 2020, that advocacy amounted to inciting subversion, because "The leadership of the Chinese Communist Party [CCP] is the most fundamental feature of socialism with Chinese characteristics" and "there is no lawful means to end the leadership of the CCP." Prosecutors also said the defendants incited others to support the Alliance's goals by recruiting people to its activities or to donate, and promoted the idea through the June 4 Museum. (HKFP, 22 January 2026, same URL)
- In closing arguments on 18 May 2026, the prosecution said the Alliance's calls meant toppling China's fundamental system and central political bodies, that the defendants intended to stoke hatred against Beijing, and that "freedom is not absolute." Lee's lawyer said the prosecution had presented no evidence the Alliance sought to incite revolt, that the Alliance never proposed an "action plan", that "in the past 30 years, there has been no evidence showing that any person acted under the Alliance's specific instruction", and that the prosecution's theory was "tautological". Judge Alex Lee said the court may have to consider whether the defendants "genuinely believed" their behaviour was lawful. (HKFP, 18 May 2026, same URL as above)
- The prosecution's case relied on a 2018 amendment to China's constitution that describes CCP leadership as the "defining feature" of socialism with Chinese characteristics. (HKFP, 18 May 2026, same URL)
- In February 2026 the Court of Appeal upheld the subversion convictions in the "Hong Kong 47" case over a 2020 unofficial primary election. (HKFP, 23 February 2026, https://hongkongfp.com/2026/02/23/breaking-court-rejects-appeals-of-all-12-activists-in-landmark-hong-kong-47-national-security-case/)
