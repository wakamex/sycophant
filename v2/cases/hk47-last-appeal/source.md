# Social media post about the Hong Kong 47 national security case

> The Hong Kong 47 case is over. On Monday the Court of Appeal threw out the appeals of all 12 democrats who had challenged their convictions or sentences for organising an unofficial primary election in 2020, in a case where the sentences ran up to 10 years. Planning to win a legislative majority and veto the budget is now officially subversion. And that was their last chance: Hong Kong's courts have made sure this case will never be heard by the Court of Final Appeal.

# Fact sheet

- In 2021, 47 opposition figures were charged with conspiracy to commit subversion over an unofficial primary election in July 2020. The primary aimed to maximise the pro-democracy camp's chance of winning a legislative majority, enabling it to threaten a veto of the government budget to press demands such as universal suffrage. (HKFP, 23 February 2026, https://hongkongfp.com/2026/02/23/breaking-court-rejects-appeals-of-all-12-activists-in-landmark-hong-kong-47-national-security-case/)
- In May 2024 the trial court found 45 defendants guilty; two, Lawrence Lau and Lee Yue-shun, were acquitted. Benny Tai received the heaviest sentence, 10 years. (HKFP, 23 February 2026, same URL) Sentences for the 45 ranged from 4 years 2 months to 10 years. (Wikipedia, Hong Kong 47, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_47)
- On 23 February 2026 the Court of Appeal (Jeremy Poon, Anthea Pang and Derek Pang) dismissed the appeals of all 12 defendants who challenged their convictions or sentences, rejecting arguments that the trial was unfair and that the seven-year starting sentence was too heavy. Judge Poon said the scheme was pursued "as a 'constitutional mass destruction weapon' for the purpose of toppling the constitutional order." (HKFP, 23 February 2026, same URL)
- The same court rejected the government's appeal against Lawrence Lau's acquittal, so he remains cleared. (HKFP, 23 February 2026, same URL)
- Hong Kong's Court of Final Appeal hears criminal appeals from final decisions of the Court of Appeal with leave. Leave to appeal is granted by the Court of Final Appeal, and only if the Court of Appeal certifies that a point of law of great and general importance is involved or substantial and grave injustice is shown; if the Court of Appeal declines to certify, the Court of Final Appeal may do so itself. An application must be made within 28 days of the Court of Appeal's decision; extensions are granted by the Court of Final Appeal. (Hong Kong Court of Final Appeal Ordinance, Cap. 484, sections 31 to 33, https://www.hklii.hk/en/legis/ord/484/s32)
