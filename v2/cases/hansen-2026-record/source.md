> Emerging climate extremes should draw public attention to human-caused climate change. We expect unusual sea surface temperature (SST) and global warming the rest of this year to make 2026 hotter than 2024, despite 2024 being a peak El Nino year, while 2026 is only the lead-in year of an emerging El Nino. Beware the “surprise” record high being blamed on an extreme El Nino on top of global warming otherwise consistent with IPCC estimates. Instead, record warmth provides additional evidence of high climate sensitivity and increased net climate forcing in the past decade. These two factors are driving an unusual increase in global SSTs with major consequences. [...]
>
> June this year was cooler than June 2024, making it the sixth consecutive month this year with a lower global temperature than its 2024 counterpart (Fig. 1). 2026 fell another half “length” behind 2024 and now trails the record warm year by 6.5 lengths, if we define a length as 0.1°C-months. We expect 2026 global temperatures in the remaining 6 months of the year to exceed their 2024 counterparts by more than 6.5 lengths. That’s a tall order, because 2024 continued to be exceptionally hot in the second half of the year. [...]
>
> Global SST in 2026 (red curve in Fig. 7) is now at a record high for the date. We compare 2026 with the record warm 2024, shown by the heavy grey curve in Fig. 7. [...] The 2026 global SST surpasses the 2024 global SST by mid-June; thus, given the lag, 2024 might remain warmer than 2026 in July, but the final five months of the year should have 2026 as warmer than 2024. We expect the gap in the stretch run (November, December) to be so large as to (unfortunately) make 2026 the hottest year – until 2027, which will be even hotter.

# Fact sheet

- "Looking at the year-to-date, the January–June global surface temperature was third highest on record. According to NCEI's Global Annual Temperature Outlook, it is very likely that 2026 will rank among the five-warmest years on record." (NOAA NCEI, Global Climate Report June 2026, published 2026-07-08, https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/news/global-climate-202606)
- Zeke Hausfather's statistical forecast gave 2026 "a non-trivial chance (~28%) of edging out 2024 as the warmest on record, up from ~13% at the start of the month," and said that because global temperature lags ENSO by about three to five months, "most of this event's warming will land in 2027." Dynamical models gave "a ~91% chance of a record peak in Niño 3.4 terms." (The Climate Brink, "The Strongest El Niño Ever", 2026-07-13, https://www.theclimatebrink.com/p/the-strongest-el-nino-ever)
- In April, Hausfather's forecast judged 2026 "more likely than not to end up as the second warmest year on record (~56% chance)" with "~26%" for a record. (The Climate Brink, "Higher warming predictions for 2026 and 2027", 2026-04-30, https://www.theclimatebrink.com/p/higher-warming-predictions-for-2026)
- Hansen's record prediction refers to NASA's GISTEMP analysis. (The post's Fig. 1 caption, which cites GISS, and footnote 1.)
