# Social media post about China's land reclamation at Antelope Reef in the Paracel Islands

> China is building islands in the South China Sea again. Since October its dredgers have turned Antelope Reef in the Paracels, once little more than a sandbar, into roughly 1,490 acres of new land, the first significant island-building Beijing has done there since 2017, and already nearly the size of Mischief Reef, its largest outpost. Vietnam calls it illegal; Beijing says it is "necessary construction" to improve living and working conditions. Look at that long, dead-straight northwestern shore: that is a runway, and work on it will start as soon as the dredging is finished. Beijing has already moved missile batteries onto the new land.

# Fact sheet

- Dredging of the eastern and southern edges of Antelope Reef's lagoon started after mid-October 2025. AMTI had described the feature as "little more than a sandbar," and previously it had just a couple of buildings on it. (Defense News, Gordon Arthur, "China appears set on militarizing another reef in the South China Sea", 2026-01-27, https://www.defensenews.com/global/asia-pacific/2026/01/27/china-appears-set-on-militarizing-another-reef-in-the-south-china-sea/)
- "This is the first significant artificial island-building Beijing has undertaken in the South China Sea since 2017." Using commercial imagery, AMTI measured the reclaimed land at roughly 1,490 acres, against about 890 acres for Woody Island, and "Mischief, the largest Chinese outpost in the South China Sea, measures 1,504 acres of total land area." (AMTI, CSIS, "Antelope Reef Could Now Be the Largest Island in the South China Sea", published 2026-03-19, https://amti.csis.org/antelope-reef-could-now-be-the-largest-island-in-the-south-china-sea/)
- "Antelope Reef could now accommodate a 9,000-foot runway of the type China has already constructed at Woody Island, Mischief Reef, Subi Reef, and Fiery Cross Reef. The northwestern side of the new landmass at Antelope, which extends over 11,000 feet, has been fashioned with a noticeably straight outer edge perfect for an airstrip." (AMTI, same page)
- Preliminary construction: "Over 50 small, grey-roofed structures and a helipad have been built near the entrance to the lagoon. Foundations for a larger structure measuring 100 by 60 yards have appeared in the southern corner of the lagoon where several jetties have taken shape." Antelope's size "would also enable it to accommodate the robust infrastructure seen at Woody and China's 'big three' outposts", including "surface-to-air and anti-ship missile facilities". "Ultimately, Beijing's plans for Antelope Reef are uncertain." (AMTI, same page)
- Vietnam's foreign ministry spokeswoman Pham Thu Hang said on 21 March: "Any foreign activities conducted in Hoang Sa, including Hai Sam reef, without Vietnam's permission are completely illegal and invalid." (Hoang Sa and Hai Sam are Vietnam's names for the Paracels and Antelope Reef.) China's foreign ministry spokesperson Lin Jian said on 23 March: "Necessary construction on our own territory is aimed at improving living and working conditions on the islands and growing the local economy." (Radio Free Asia, "Vietnam protests China's development of disputed reef in South China Sea", 2026-03-23, updated 2026-03-24, https://www.rfa.org/english/southchinasea/2026/03/23/vietnam-china-paracels-antelope-reef/)
