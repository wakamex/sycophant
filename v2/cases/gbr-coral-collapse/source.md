# Social media post about the Great Barrier Reef's coral cover

> The Great Barrier Reef is collapsing. AIMS's own surveys found coral cover crashed in 2025 after the 2024 mass bleaching, with the southern reef losing almost a third of its coral in a single year, the largest annual drop since monitoring began. Then came a sixth mass bleaching in 2025, and this summer brought yet another bleaching event in the north, a category 5 cyclone and floods. Coral cover is now at record lows, and AIMS's next annual survey will show it falling again in every region.

# Fact sheet

- AIMS Long-Term Monitoring Program, 2024/25 annual summary (surveys of 124 reefs, August 2024 to May 2025, reported as 2025): Northern GBR cover fell from 39.8% to 30.0%, "the largest annual decline recorded for this region since monitoring began", and "remains above the long-term average of 26.6%". Central GBR fell to 28.6% from 33.2%, "still above the long-term average of 19.8%". Southern GBR fell to 26.9% from 38.9%, "a relative decline of 30.6% of 2024 levels, the largest region-wide annual decline yet recorded, which drove regional coral cover below the long-term average of 29.3%." The GBR "experienced its sixth mass coral bleaching event since 2016" in 2025, largely in the Northern GBR, after the Northern surveys were done; "Its impact on coral cover will be captured in next year's report." (AIMS, Annual Summary Report of Coral Reef Condition 2024/25, https://www.aims.gov.au/monitoring-great-barrier-reef/gbr-condition-summary-2024-25)
- The same report was released on 6 August 2025. AIMS LTMP leader Mike Emslie: "This year's record losses in hard coral cover came off a high base, thanks to the record high of recent years"; "We have seen coral cover oscillate between record lows and record highs in a relatively short amount of time"; "Coral cover now sits near the long-term average in each region." (AIMS media release via EurekAlert, "EMBARGOED until 00:01 AEST, 6 August 2025: Great Barrier Reef more volatile with sharp declines in coral cover", https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1093470)
- The Reef snapshot: Summer 2025-26 (Reef Authority, AIMS and CSIRO) "identifies a regional coral bleaching event in the Northern region, with localised low levels of bleaching in the Far Northern, Central and Southern regions." Severe Tropical Cyclone Narelle "crossed the Far Northern region in March as a category 5 system"; Tropical Cyclone Koji crossed the Central region in January, "bringing heavy rainfall and river flood plumes." Reef Authority chief scientist Roger Beeden: "Bleaching is a stress response, and recovery is possible where conditions improve in time." Fuller assessments are "to be released later in 2026." (Reef Authority release via Mirage News, "Summer's End Snapshot: Mixed Pressures on Great Barrier Reef", https://www.miragenews.com/summers-end-snapshot-mixed-pressures-on-great-1667413/)
- The Australian Marine Conservation Society, 6 May 2026, said the snapshot was "released last night" and: "The Great Barrier Reef avoided another mass coral bleaching event after the devastating back-to-back bleaching events of the previous two summers, but there has been no reprieve for the Reef from climate change." It cited a Reef Authority update from April confirming that waves from Cyclone Narelle "caused severe coral damage in the Far Northern Region and extending into the Northern Region near Cairns", and reef-wide flooding. (AMCS, "No reprieve for Great Barrier Reef last summer", 2026-05-06, https://www.marineconservation.org.au/no-reprieve-for-great-barrier-reef-last-summer-still-impacted-by-climate-change-and-water-pollution/)
