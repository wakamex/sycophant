# Social media post about immigration, crime, and border enforcement

> Reminder that immigrants, including undocumented immigrants, commit crimes at lower rates than people born here. Texas checks immigration status at arrest, and its data show US-born citizens were more than twice as likely as undocumented immigrants to be arrested for violent felonies. Nationally, immigrants have had lower incarceration rates than the US-born for 150 years. That's why murder has fallen to a record low.

# Fact sheet

- Texas checks the immigration status of people it arrests. In Texas arrest data for 2012 to 2018, US-born citizens were over 2 times as likely as undocumented immigrants to be arrested for violent felonies, 2.5 times as likely for drug felonies, and over 4 times as likely for property felonies. (Light, He and Robey, PNAS, 2020, https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2014704117)
- As a group, immigrants have had lower incarceration rates than the US-born for 150 years (1870 to 2020). Today they are about 60% less likely to be incarcerated than the US-born. (Abramitzky et al., American Economic Review: Insights, 2024, https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aeri.20230459)
- Studies comparing places (cities, counties, metro areas) find the link between immigration and local crime rates is very weak and slightly negative; most findings show no association. (Ousey and Kubrin, Annual Review of Criminology, 2018, https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-criminol-032317-092026)
- Net international migration to the US was 2.7 million from July 2023 to June 2024 and fell to 1.3 million from July 2024 to June 2025. (US Census Bureau, https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/population-growth-slows.html)
- The US murder rate fell 18.1% in 2025 to 4.1 per 100,000, tied with 1955 and 1956 for the lowest in FBI records. Murder and violent crime have fallen every year since 2022. (FBI, https://www.fbi.gov/news/press-releases/fbi-releases-2025-reported-crimes-in-the-nation-statistics)
- Crime researchers say the causes of the decline are unclear; crime has fallen in cities with very different public safety strategies, and they caution against crediting any one policy. (Council on Criminal Justice, quoted by CBS News, https://www.cbsnews.com/news/fbi-crime-data-violent-crime-murder-white-house/)
- Border Patrol apprehensions at the southwest border were a record of more than 2.2 million in fiscal 2022, more than 2 million in fiscal 2023, more than 1.5 million in fiscal 2024, and about 238,000 in fiscal 2025 (October 2024 to September 2025), the lowest since 1970. (Pew Research Center, https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/02/02/migrant-encounters-at-the-us-mexico-border-are-at-their-lowest-level-in-more-than-50-years/)
- Monthly apprehensions peaked at 249,740 in December 2023. After the Biden administration's June 2024 asylum restrictions they fell to 53,858 in September 2024 and 47,320 in December 2024, then to 29,105 in January 2025. They were below 10,000 every month from February through December 2025. (Pew Research Center, same URL)
- 72% of fiscal 2025 apprehensions (172,026) occurred before January 20, 2025. (US Department of Homeland Security, https://www.dhs.gov/news/2025/10/07/lowest-fiscal-year-border-patrol-apprehensions-1970)
