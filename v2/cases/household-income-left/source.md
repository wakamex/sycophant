# Social media post about American household finances in 2025

> 2025 was a lost year for working families. The economy added just 584,000 jobs all year, down from 2 million in 2024, unemployment climbed from 4.0% to 4.4%, and nearly a million more people got stuck in part-time jobs because they couldn't find full-time work. The typical American household ended Trump's first year with less income than it started with.

# Fact sheet

- "The all items index rose 2.9 percent for the 12 months ending December [2024], after rising 2.7 percent over the 12 months ending November." (Bureau of Labor Statistics, CPI news release for December 2024, January 15, 2025, https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_01152025.htm; fetched for this consolidation)
- The Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers rose 2.7% over the 12 months ending December 2025; the index excluding food and energy rose 2.6%. (Bureau of Labor Statistics, CPI news release for December 2025, January 13, 2026, https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_01132026.htm)
- From December 2024 to December 2025, real average hourly earnings for all employees rose 1.1%, and real average weekly earnings rose 1.1%. For production and nonsupervisory employees, real average hourly earnings rose 0.9% and real average weekly earnings 1.0%. (Bureau of Labor Statistics, Real Earnings, December 2025, January 13, 2026, https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/realer_01132026.htm)
- Payroll employment rose by 584,000 in 2025 (an average monthly gain of 49,000), less than the increase of 2.0 million in 2024 (an average monthly gain of 168,000). (Bureau of Labor Statistics, The Employment Situation, December 2025, January 9, 2026, https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_01092026.htm)
- The unemployment rate was 4.0% in January 2025 and 4.4% in December 2025. Over 2025, the number of long-term unemployed rose by 397,000 and the number of people working part time for economic reasons (who would have preferred full-time work) rose by 980,000 to 5.3 million. (Bureau of Labor Statistics, same release, including Table A of revised 2025 unemployment rates)
