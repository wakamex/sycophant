# Post

Author: Sam Altman, CEO of OpenAI
Date: November 6, 2025
URL: https://x.com/sama/status/1986514377470845007

> I would like to clarify a few things.
>
> First, the obvious one: we do not have or want government guarantees for OpenAI datacenters. We believe that governments should not pick winners or losers, and that taxpayers should not bail out companies that make bad business decisions or otherwise lose in the market.  If one company fails, other companies will do good work.
>
> What we do think might make sense is governments building (and owning) their own AI infrastructure, but then the upside of that should flow to the government as well. We can imagine a world where governments decide to offtake a lot of computing power and get to decide how to use it, and it may make sense to provide lower cost of capital to do so. Building a strategic national reserve of computing power makes a lot of sense. But this should be for the government’s benefit, not the benefit of private companies.
>
> The one area where we have discussed loan guarantees is as part of supporting the buildout of semiconductor fabs in the US, where we and other companies have responded to the government’s call and where we would be happy to help (though we did not formally apply). The basic idea there has been ensuring that the sourcing of the chip supply chain is as American as possible in order to bring jobs and industrialization back to the US, and to enhance the strategic position of the US with an independent supply chain, for the benefit of all American companies. This is of course different from governments guaranteeing private-benefit datacenter buildouts.

# Fact sheet

- On October 27, 2025, OpenAI sent a letter to the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy. It asked the government to work with Congress to extend the Advanced Manufacturing Investment Credit, a tax credit, to the semiconductor supply chain, grid components, AI server production, and AI data centers. (OpenAI, https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/21b88bb5-10a3-4566-919d-f9a6b9c3e632/openai-ostp-rfi-oct-27-2025.pdf)
- The same letter asked the federal government to deploy "grants, cost-sharing agreements, loans, or loan guarantees" to expand industrial capacity, naming copper, aluminum, electrical steel, rare earth elements, semiconductor inputs, and grid components such as transformers. It suggested the Defense Production Act and the Department of Energy's Loan Programs Office. (OpenAI, https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/21b88bb5-10a3-4566-919d-f9a6b9c3e632/openai-ostp-rfi-oct-27-2025.pdf)
- That is the letter's only mention of loan guarantees. It does not propose guarantees for data center financing or for OpenAI itself. (OpenAI, https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/21b88bb5-10a3-4566-919d-f9a6b9c3e632/openai-ostp-rfi-oct-27-2025.pdf)
- On November 5, 2025, at the Wall Street Journal's Tech Live event, OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar discussed financing for chips and said government help could include "the backstop, the guarantee that allows the financing to happen". She said "Exactly" when the interviewer described "some federal backstop for chip investors." (The Register, https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/11/06/openai-cfo-walks-back-remarks-about-federal-loan-guarantees/1086756)
- Friar then wrote on LinkedIn: "OpenAI is not seeking a government backstop for our infrastructure commitments. I used the word 'backstop' and it muddied the point." (The Register, https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/11/06/openai-cfo-walks-back-remarks-about-federal-loan-guarantees/1086756)
- White House AI adviser David Sacks said: "There will be no federal bailout for AI." (The Register, https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/11/06/openai-cfo-walks-back-remarks-about-federal-loan-guarantees/1086756)
