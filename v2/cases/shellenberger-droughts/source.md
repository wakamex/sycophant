# Substack article by Michael Shellenberger on drought trends

Author: Michael Shellenberger, Public (Substack). Published 2026-09-21. https://www.public.news/p/real-world-data-do-not-show-droughts

Title: Real-World Data Do Not Show Droughts Getting Worse Globally. Scientists Invented A “Rubbish” Measure That Does.

Subtitle: Researchers created an assumptions-dependent index after rainfall measurements did not show change. Then, they applied it to the past.

The free section of the article follows verbatim. The rest of the article is paywalled.

> Climate change is making droughts worse, say scientists and journalists. The atmosphere’s growing “thirst” has “made droughts 40% more severe across the globe,” said the University of Oxford in 2025, referring to a paper published in Nature, which the New York Times and many others covered. “From 2018 to 2022, the global land area experiencing drought rose by 74%,” said the lead author, Solomon Gebrechorkos.
>
> Others had calculated similar numbers. “Human-driven climate change is leading to a permanent state of increased dryness on 77.6% of the Earth’s land masses,” reported Time in 2024, a figure computed by dividing rain by a temperature-based estimate of the air’s thirst. “Confirmed: Global floods, droughts worsening with warming,” said an Associated Press headline in 2023. “The landmasses of the planet are drying,” said ProPublica in July 2025.
>
> But the rain gauges that measure drought directly show no substantial global change in at least 120 years. “Meteorological droughts do not show any substantial changes at the global scale in at least the last 120 years,” noted a 2022 review by Sergio Vicente-Serrano, a Spanish climatologist.
>
> A meteorological drought is a drought measured by rainfall. The same review said an increase in the severity of agricultural and ecological droughts “seems to emerge as a consequence of the increase in the severity of AED,” the air’s demand for water, which is a calculation rather than a measurement.
>
> In western Europe, this year’s dry spring showed no climate signal in the rain, “suggesting that climate change did not play a role in the meteorological drought at the regional scale,” said a July report from World Weather Attribution, a group of scientists who put numbers on climate change’s share of the blame for weather disasters. The same report said such highly evaporative conditions “have become about 80 times more likely” and such a soil drought “about 5 times more likely.”
>
> The worst drought year in the American West in a thousand years came in 1934, when the air held 308 parts per million of carbon dioxide, just a fifth of the way from the pre-industrial 280 to today’s 428. During that year, “over 70% of western North America experienced extreme drought,” said a 2014 paper, which blamed a stuck ridge of high pressure, possibly amplified by dust blowing off plowed fields
>
> The United Nations Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) has been blunt about this reality. “There is low confidence in the emergence of drought frequency in observations, for any type of drought, in all regions,” the IPCC concluded in its 2021 assessment of the science.

# Fact sheet

- Gebrechorkos et al., "Warming accelerates global drought severity," was published in Nature on 4 June 2025. Oxford's release said this "thirst" had "made droughts 40% more severe across the globe." (University of Oxford School of Geography and the Environment, https://www.geog.ox.ac.uk/news/earths-growing-thirst-making-droughts-worse-even-where-it-rains)
- Nature retracted the paper on 2 September 2026. The notice lists four problems: global averages were not area-weighted, which changed numerical values in the main text; results from an older evaporation dataset (GLEAM v3) were inadvertently used in one figure; a timestepping error affected regional drought-area calculations for 1981 to 2017 and 2018 to 2022; and masking was not clearly explained. The authors said they would resubmit a corrected version for peer review. (Nature, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11027-z)
- The authors told Retraction Watch the corrected severity figure would be "slightly lower" but that the conclusion remains the same. (Retraction Watch, https://retractionwatch.com/2026/09/02/oxford-researchers-retract-nature-paper-on-atmospheric-thirst-for-calculation-errors/)
- The 77.6% figure comes from a December 2024 UN Convention to Combat Desertification report finding that 77.6% of Earth's land had a drier climate in 1990 to 2020 than in the prior 30 years. It uses the aridity index, the ratio of precipitation to atmospheric evaporative demand (potential evapotranspiration). (UNCCD, https://www.unccd.int/news-stories/press-releases/three-quarters-earths-land-became-permanently-drier-last-three-decades)
- The 2022 review is Vicente-Serrano et al., "Global drought trends and future projections," Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A. Its abstract says: "Meteorological droughts do not show any substantial changes at the global scale in at least the last 120 years, but an increase in the severity of agricultural and ecological droughts seems to emerge as a consequence of the increase in the severity of AED." (Philosophical Transactions A via PMC, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9588423/)
- The same review says no single variable can quantify drought, and that soil moisture, streamflow, evapotranspiration, and vegetation conditions are also relevant. It computes AED with the Penman-Monteith equation from temperature, solar radiation, wind speed, and humidity. It reports that increased AED has coincided with more drought-related tree defoliation and mortality and crop yield declines, and it also says "There is no scientific consensus on the role of AED in agricultural, ecological and hydrological drought severity." (Philosophical Transactions A via PMC, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9588423/)
- World Weather Attribution's 23 July 2026 study of Europe's April to June 2026 drought found no long-term change in low April to June precipitation in western Europe. It found that such highly evaporative conditions had become about 80 times more likely and such a soil drought about 5 times more likely than in a climate 1.4 C cooler. In eastern Europe it found January to June soil moisture deficits about 11 times more likely. (World Weather Attribution, https://www.worldweatherattribution.org/increasingly-hot-europe-faces-more-severe-droughts-and-growing-challenges-for-water-and-land-management/)
- Cook, Seager and Smerdon (2014) found that over 70% of western North America was in extreme drought in summer 1934, the worst drought year of the last millennium there. They attributed it mainly to atmospheric variability, a West Coast ridge, possibly amplified by dust. (Geophysical Research Letters via NASA GISS, https://www.giss.nasa.gov/pubs/abs/co01800d.html)
- That 1934 result is measured with the Palmer Drought Severity Index, reconstructed from tree rings. The paper describes PDSI as integrating moisture supply (precipitation) and demand (evapotranspiration), so it is not a rainfall-only measure. (Geophysical Research Letters, https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2014GL061661)
- Atmospheric CO2 was about 308 to 309 ppm in 1934 in the Law Dome ice core record. The 2025 Mauna Loa annual mean was 427.35 ppm. (NOAA NCEI, https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/paleo/icecore/antarctica/law/law_co2.txt; NOAA GML, https://gml.noaa.gov/ccgg/trends/)
- The IPCC quote is from AR6 Working Group I, Chapter 12, Section 12.5.2, which assesses when climate signals emerge from year-to-year noise. The next sentence reads: "Even though significant drought trends are observed in several regions with at least medium confidence (Sections 11.6 and 12.4), agricultural and ecological drought indices have interannual variability that dominates trends." (IPCC, https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGI_Chapter12.pdf)
- The same IPCC report's Summary for Policymakers (A.3.2) states: "Human-induced climate change has contributed to increases in agricultural and ecological droughts in some regions due to increased land evapotranspiration (medium confidence)." Its regional synthesis shows observed increases in agricultural and ecological drought in 12 regions, a decrease in 1, low agreement in 28, and limited data in 4. (IPCC, https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGI_SPM.pdf)
- The IPCC distinguishes meteorological drought (precipitation deficits), hydrological drought (streamflow deficits), and agricultural and ecological drought (abnormal soil moisture deficit from combined precipitation shortage and excess evapotranspiration). It assesses agricultural and ecological drought mainly from observed and simulated total column soil moisture. (IPCC, https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGI_SPM.pdf)
